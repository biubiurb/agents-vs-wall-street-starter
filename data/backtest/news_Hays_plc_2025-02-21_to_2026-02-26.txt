HEADLINE: Hays cuts FY25 profit expectation to about £45m after a broad-based deterioration in permanent recruitment
CATEGORY: COMPANY
IMPACT SCORE: 5/5
FINANCIAL STATEMENT LINE(S): revenue/sales (net fees), operating expense, operating profit, profit before tax, EPS, working capital, cash flow
WHY IT COULD AFFECT EARNINGS: This was a material profit warning: Hays expected Q4 like-for-like net fees to fall 9%, including a 14% permanent-fee decline, and cut expected FY25 pre-exceptional operating profit to about £45 million. Germany was expected to decline 5%, with a subdued automotive sector weighing on temp and permanent hiring; UK&I and ANZ permanent markets had also weakened.

It said the pension buy-in would be materially positive for free cash flow from FY26.

CATEGORY: MACRO
IMPACT SCORE: 3/5
FINANCIAL STATEMENT LINE(S): revenue/sales (net fees), operating profit, working capital, cash flow
WHY IT COULD AFFECT EARNINGS: Recruitment fees depend on employer vacancy creation, placements and, for temporary staffing, assignments and hours worked. The agency's evidence that German labor demand and registered vacancies declined materially signals a lower addressable volume of new placements in Hays' largest market. Lower placements and hours would reduce net fees and operating profit, while changing temporary payroll and receivables balances can affect working capital and operating cash flow.
CONTENT (Summary from accessible article): Germany's Federal Employment Agency said labor demand continued to decline during 2025 amid weak economic development. Average registered vacancies were 632,000, down 62,000 from 2024, while total reported jobs fell to 1.461 million, the lowest level in 25 years. The agency also reported higher unemployment and an elevated use of short-time work.
